# ZERO build challenge — product notes

## The Middle Market

Small businesses often run customer operations through WhatsApp, answering the same questions about prices, availability, bookings, and delivery repeatedly. Information gets buried in chats, replies take time, and a missed message can mean a lost customer. I’d first build a simple WhatsApp assistant for one type of business, such as a photography studio. It would use the owner’s actual price list, services, and availability to draft accurate replies to common questions, while asking the owner to approve anything uncertain. I’d start with the ten most frequent questions rather than trying to automate every conversation. The first measure of success would be whether the owner spends less time answering repetitive messages without losing control of customer interactions.

## CLOSURE

Job rejection is difficult, but silence or a generic rejection email leaves applicants guessing what to improve. That uncertainty can follow them into dozens of future applications. CLOSURE addresses this by offering personalised, human-written feedback rather than an arbitrary score. My first improvement would be a focused submission flow that collects the job description, the applicant’s resume, and the stage at which they were rejected, then gives the reviewer everything needed to write useful feedback. The resulting answer should separate what is supported by the available evidence from what is an informed inference, and finish with one concrete action the applicant can take. I’d measure whether applicants find the feedback useful enough to make a real change before applying again.

## ISHQweds

Wedding planning involves many decisions shared between couples and their families, from guest lists and budgets to vendors and scheduling. Different expectations can turn small disagreements into stressful conversations, while details become scattered across chats and calls. I’d first build one shared wedding workspace with a common budget, guest list, vendor shortlist, and clear record of decisions. Each decision would have an owner, a deadline, and a simple way to record agreement or disagreement. Rather than building an enormous wedding-planning platform immediately, I’d test this with a single couple and their families to find out which decisions cause the most friction. The goal would be to reduce confusion and repeated arguments so the couple can spend more time enjoying their wedding.
